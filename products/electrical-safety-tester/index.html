FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a hi-pot test for batteries?

A hi-pot (dielectric withstand) test applies a high voltage (typically 500–5000 V DC or AC) between the battery's live conductors and its enclosure or external terminals to verify insulation integrity. It ensures that the battery can withstand voltage transients without dangerous breakdown or arc-over.

What insulation resistance values are acceptable?

For EV and ESS battery packs, insulation resistance should be ≥500 Ω/V of the pack's nominal voltage (e.g., ≥192 kΩ for a 384 V pack) per IEC 62133 and GB/T 31485. For consumer cells, ≥100 MΩ at 500 V DC is typical. The VoltTest electrical safety tester measures from 0.01 Ω to 10 GΩ with ±2% accuracy.

Can the tester be integrated into an EOL production line?

Yes. The VoltTest electrical safety tester is designed for automated EOL integration with PLC trigger inputs, pass/fail outputs, and RS-485/Ethernet communication. It can be combined with pack test systems to perform insulation and hi-pot tests as part of the automated EOL sequence.

What safety standards require electrical safety testing?

Electrical safety testing is mandated by IEC 62133-2, GB/T 31485, GB 38031, UL 1973 and UN 38.3 for lithium battery packs. These standards require insulation resistance, dielectric withstand and continuity verification as part of the type test and production safety verification.

What is the difference between dielectric withstand and insulation resistance testing?

Insulation resistance testing applies a moderate DC voltage (e.g., 500 V) and measures the resistance value (in MΩ or GΩ) — it quantifies insulation quality. Dielectric withstand (hi-pot) testing applies a higher voltage (e.g., 1500 V AC) for a set duration and checks for breakdown — it verifies safety margin. Both are complementary and both are performed in battery EOL testing.
