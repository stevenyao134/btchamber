Battery Environment & Climate Test Chambers

Temperature, humidity and thermal shock chambers from 80 L benchtop units to walk-in rooms — the environmental half of a battery qualification programme.

Environmental Simulation

Reproduce Field Conditions Inside a Controlled Volume

A battery that passes at room temperature can still fail in a vehicle roof in summer or a container in winter. VoltTest chambers hold temperature and humidity to tight tolerances across the whole working volume, so a cell in the corner ages under the same conditions as one in the centre — which is what makes a cycling result repeatable.

  • ✓ Temperature range −70 °C to +150 °C standard, −40 °C to +180 °C high-temperature option
  • ✓ Humidity 20–98 % RH with ±2 % RH stability for damp heat and 85/85 runs
  • ✓ Uniformity ±0.5 °C verified on a nine-point grid at FAT, not just at the control sensor
  • ✓ Ramp rates 1–5 °C/min heating and 1–3 °C/min cooling, programmable per segment
  • ✓ Compressor and heater sized for 1000 h+ continuous cycling without derating

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an environmental chamber and a thermal shock chamber?

An environmental chamber changes temperature inside one working volume at a controlled ramp rate, typically 1–5 °C/min, and holds the setpoint for a defined dwell time. A thermal shock chamber moves the sample between two separately controlled zones (usually −70 °C and +150 °C) with transfer times under one minute, which produces the abrupt step change required by IEC 60068-2-14 Na. Choose the single-volume chamber for UN 38.3 T2, GB 38031 and IEC 62660-2 cycles; choose two-zone thermal shock where the standard specifies a transfer time.

What temperature uniformity can VoltTest guarantee?

Standard models are validated at ±0.5 °C in the empty chamber across a nine-point grid at FAT, with ±2 % RH humidity stability. With a loaded fixture the achievable gradient depends on the heat dissipated by the samples and the free air path around them, so we size the air circulation and shelf spacing against your expected load rather than quoting an empty-chamber figure only.

What chamber specification is needed for 85/85 damp heat testing?

85 °C / 85 % RH for 1000 h (IEC 60068-2-78 steady state) requires steam humidification with demineralised water, a heated water reservoir and a chamber liner that resists condensation dripping onto the samples. Insist on continuous water supply or a reservoir sized for unattended weekend running, plus an independent humidity sensor, because a single wet-bulb sensor that drifts will silently invalidate a 1000 h run.

Can the chamber be combined with a battery cycler for in-chamber charge and discharge?

Yes. Feed-through ports with sealed cable glands let charge/discharge leads and voltage-sense wires enter the working volume without breaking the thermal seal. VoltTest supplies port plates from 50 mm to 200 mm and can pre-route current busbars for packs up to several hundred amps, sized so the internal temperature rise of the conductors does not disturb the chamber setpoint.

What safety provisions are required when testing lithium cells in a temperature chamber?

A lithium cell can vent or go into thermal runaway inside the chamber, so specify a stainless steel liner, solvent-resistant door seals, a mechanical pressure relief flap, gas purge or extraction and an independent over-temperature cut-out that is separate from the control thermocouple. For abuse testing add reinforced doors, fire suppression and gas analysis; for pure environmental cycling the minimum is pressure relief plus extraction, because a sealed chamber containing a venting cell becomes a pressure vessel.
